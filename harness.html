The harness

How Turing Agent works

Verifying a research result means more than proving a theorem whose formal statement is handed to you. Papers bring their own definitions, conventions and intermediate results, and every one of them must be translated faithfully before proof search can even start. Turing Agent is built around that whole process.

Three requirements

Every formal development has to satisfy three requirements at once. Most automated provers address only the last one, starting from statements that are already formalized.

Faithfulness

Preserve the original claim

The Lean statement must keep the domains, quantifiers, assumptions and conclusion of the natural-language claim. A statement can compile and still say something else.

Validity

Pass Lean's checks

Syntax, type and elaboration errors must be resolved through iterative debugging so Lean produces a valid proof term.

Completeness

Leave no gaps

Every proof obligation is discharged — no sorry — across long developments with many dependent auxiliary results.

Design

A two-scale architecture

Turing Agent separates theorem formalization from proof formalization. The main theorem is formalized and reviewed before proof construction begins, and stays fixed afterwards. Intermediate claims go through the same check selectively while the proof is being built. Agents are grounded in Mathlib and the domain library CSLib.

01

Theorem formalization (TF) agent

Searches Mathlib and CSLib for suitable definitions, introduces new ones where the libraries fall short, and writes the Lean statement. After Lean accepts it, the agent reviews whether domains, assumptions, quantifier dependencies and conclusion match the source — comparing the text against the statement, its elaborated type and the full bodies of new definitions — and revises until both checks pass.

02

Proof decomposition into a DAG

Guided by the informal proof, a proof formalization (PF) agent splits a goal into intermediate claims and records their dependencies in a directed acyclic graph. A split is accepted only after Lean verifies that the child statements, taken as assumptions, imply the parent. The procedure recurses; goals that can be proved directly are not split.

03

Parallel construction

Independent child goals are assigned to several PF agents at once. Each revises or further decomposes its claim from Lean feedback; completed branches are rechecked before the checked derivation closes the parent, continuing upward to the main theorem.

04

Selective semantic review

The decomposition check proves that children imply the parent, not that they match the informal argument. Reviewing every claim would be costly, so PF agents request a TF review only when a new concept's meaning is uncertain, assumptions or quantifiers are unclear, or a reformulated claim may change scope.

05

Experience extraction & reuse

Compressing long interaction histories loses findings. PF agents extract evidence-backed records — useful library lemmas, required side conditions, causes and fixes of failures — merge them per claim, and receive the relevant records after each context compaction.

06

Final verification

An independent Lean kernel rechecks the complete proof against the fixed statement (with an axiom audit). A review agent then compares the proof with the informal argument: regrouping steps is fine; omitted essential steps, unsupported assumptions or a substituted argument are flagged.

Evaluation

Benchmark results

Theorem formalization is measured on the 143 problems of Formal TCS Bench (statements from recent TCS papers). A submission succeeds only if it compiles and passes independent semantic review. Proof formalization uses 248 theorems from the PFR and TestingLowerBounds (TLB) projects in RLMEval; success means a complete proof with no sorry. Each model is run as direct generation, inside Codex CLI, inside Claude Code, and inside Turing Agent. All numbers are pass@1 with one final submission per problem.

Pass@1 (%). Underlined: best in column. PFR covers additive combinatorics and entropy methods (Polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa); TLB covers information-theoretic lower bounds for hypothesis testing.

With GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Turing Agent reaches 58.99% and 61.53% success on Formal TCS Bench — 18.43 and 32.16 points above Claude Code, the strongest baseline — with near-complete compilation (97.20% / 99.30%). The gap between compiling and being faithful is exactly why semantic review is evaluated separately.

On RLMEval it exceeds 82% overall with both models: 82.87% with GLM-5.3 (best of all methods) and 82.66% with DeepSeek-V4-Pro, 8.06 points above Codex CLI and within 4.03 points of Claude Code.

Observations

Verification can reshape the mathematics

Working through research-level proofs revealed two behaviours beyond certifying correctness.

Proof-friendly constructions

Exploiting mathematical freedom

Existential statements often admit many witnesses, some far easier to formalize than others. The agent picks constructions that turn case-heavy comparisons into uniform arithmetic — alternative mathematics optimized for machine reasoning.

Reusable by-products

More certified knowledge than needed

Turing Agent often verifies auxiliary results that do not end up in the final dependency structure. Rather than being discarded, they remain as reusable, machine-checked artifacts.
